Stash vs YNAB, Goodbudget and EveryDollar

All four are budget apps built around giving your money a plan. Here’s where Stash is different, so you can pick the one that fits.

The short version

Pick Stash if you want a zero-based envelope budget on iPhone that logs spending from your bank’s alerts without a bank login, shows the year you could retire, and doesn’t need a subscription. Pick another app if you need Android, the web, or a budget shared with a partner, which Stash doesn’t do yet.

Stash vs YNAB

Stash follows the YNAB method: zero-based budgeting, Ready to assign, cash overspending and credit card payment envelopes work the same way. The difference is how you pay and how spending gets in. YNAB is a subscription and links to your bank through a service. Stash’s budget is free, Plus can be bought once, and spending comes in from your bank’s alerts with no bank login. Stash also estimates your retirement year. YNAB runs on the web and Android too; Stash is iPhone only. You can move your YNAB budget to Stash in about a minute.

Stash vs Goodbudget

Both use envelopes. Goodbudget is built for entering spends by hand and sharing envelopes across a household’s devices. Stash logs spends for you from your bank’s alerts, adds credit card payment envelopes and a retirement year, and keeps each person’s budget in their own iCloud.

Stash vs EveryDollar

Both are zero-based. EveryDollar is from Ramsey Solutions and follows its Baby Steps plan, with bank connection in its paid tier. Stash uses envelopes that carry money over month to month, logs from bank alerts without a bank connection, and shows the year you could retire.
