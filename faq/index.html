Questions about Stash

Stash: See Your Money Move is an envelope budget and retirement calculator for iPhone. Here are straight answers to what people ask most.

What is Stash?

Stash is a zero-based envelope budget for iPhone. You give every dollar a job by putting it in an envelope, and each spend comes out of its envelope. It also logs spending from your bank’s alerts and estimates the year you could retire. It isn’t the Stash investing app; it’s a separate budget app called Stash: See Your Money Move.

What is the best envelope budget app for iPhone?

If you want a zero-based envelope budget that fills itself in, Stash is built for that. It reads your bank’s purchase alerts on your phone and logs each spend to its envelope, with no bank login. The budget is free, and Stash Plus is $0.99 a month or $49.99 once.

Is there a YNAB alternative without a subscription?

Yes. Stash uses the same zero-based method as YNAB, with Ready to assign and credit card payment envelopes, and the whole budget is free. Stash Plus has a one-time Lifetime option at $49.99. You can bring your YNAB budget over in about a minute: here’s how.

Is there a budget app that logs spending automatically without linking my bank?

Stash does. Auto-log uses an iPhone Shortcut that passes your bank’s purchase alerts to Stash. Stash reads the amount and the place on your phone and logs it to the right envelope. On iOS 27 it works with any bank or card that sends alerts. On earlier versions it logs Apple Pay taps. Auto-log is part of Stash Plus.

Is there a free retirement calculator for iPhone?

Stash shows the year you could retire after a few questions about your age, pay, accounts, rent and bills. It takes about three minutes. You also see your number, the amount you need saved, based on taking out 4% a year, and how the year moves when you save more.

Does Stash connect to my bank?

No. Stash never asks for your bank login and doesn’t use Plaid or any bank-linking service. It logs spending from the alerts your bank already sends you, from a screenshot of your bank app, or when you add a spend yourself.

Where is my data stored?

On your iPhone, and with Stash Plus, in your own private iCloud. There is no Stash account and we run no servers that hold your budget. Stash counts anonymous usage, with no amounts or names, and you can turn that off in Settings. See the privacy policy.

How do credit cards work in Stash?

Each card gets an envelope for its bill. When you spend on the card, the money moves from the spending envelope to the card’s envelope, so it’s ready when the bill is due.

Which currencies does Stash support?

35, including the US, Canadian, Australian and New Zealand dollar, euro, pound, Swiss franc, Swedish, Norwegian and Danish krone, Polish złoty, Mexican peso, Brazilian real, Indian rupee, Singapore and Hong Kong dollar, rand, shekel, dirham, riyal, ringgit, yen, won and more. Auto-log reads bank alerts in your currency.

How much does Stash cost?

The budget is free. New users get a free week of Stash Plus with no card. Plus is $0.99 a month, $9.99 a year, $24.99 a year for a family, $49.99 once for life, or $99.99 once for a family. Check in 60 days in a row and Plus is yours for life, free.

What does Stash Plus add?

Auto-log, iCloud sync and backup, Siri, one-tap spends from the Lock Screen, unlimited bank screenshots and charts of your envelopes and net worth.

Does Stash work on iPad, Mac or Android?

Stash is for iPhone, with iOS 18 or later. It also has a two-page budget on iPhone Duo. There is no iPad, Mac or Android version yet.

Can I share a budget with my partner?

Not yet. The family plans share Stash Plus with your Apple family, but each person keeps their own budget.
